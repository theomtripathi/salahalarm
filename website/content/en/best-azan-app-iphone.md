The best azan app for iPhone is the one that gets three things right: the exact prayer time for where you are, an adhan you actually hear, and a Fajr adhan that gets you out of bed. Most apps nail the first. Plenty fail the second because of how iOS handles notifications. Almost all of them fail the third.

This guide explains what to look for, why the adhan often goes quiet on iPhone, and how four popular apps compare. App Store details were last checked in late October 2026.

## What makes a good azan app on iPhone?

1. **Accurate times with the right method.** Your mosque uses a calculation method such as Muslim World League, ISNA, Umm al-Qura, Egyptian or Diyanet, and Hanafi or standard Asr. The app should let you match it.
2. **An adhan you hear on silent.** If the adhan is just a notification, silent mode, Focus and Do Not Disturb can swallow it.
3. **A real Fajr wake-up.** Ẓuhr and ʿAsr find you awake. Fajr finds you asleep, and a one-tap notification is easy to dismiss without waking.
4. **No ads in the way.** An ad between you and the Qibla or the times is a small thing that adds up five times a day.
5. **Privacy.** Location is needed for prayer times. It should not be used to track you across other apps.

## Why does the azan go quiet on iPhone?

iOS treats most app sounds as notifications. Notification sounds are short, so many azan apps play a clip or a cut-down adhan. They are also muted by silent mode and filtered by Focus modes unless the app is allowed through. That is why you can have an azan app installed and still miss the adhan.

Two fixes help with any app. First, open Settings, then Notifications, find the app, and allow sounds and, if offered, critical alerts. Second, add the app to the allowed list of any Focus you use at night.

## 1. Salah Alarm: best for not missing Fajr

Full disclosure: this is our app. Salah Alarm works out the five prayer times for your location with the major calculation methods, including Hanafi Asr, and plays the adhan with a choice of voices such as Makkah and Madinah. For Fajr it plays the adhan as an alarm that only stops when you scan your prayer mat with your phone camera, so by the time it is quiet you are already where you pray. Strict mode has no snooze. It also has a Qibla compass, a Quran reader, home screen widgets, a prayer log and SalahLock, which locks distracting apps at prayer time until you have prayed. No ads.

**Falls short if:** you want lectures, community feeds and a big content library.

## 2. Muslim Pro: best all-in-one

Muslim Pro covers prayer times, Quran, Qibla, duas and community features, and it is the most widely used of the four. The adhan arrives as a notification, the free version has ads, and Premium is a monthly or yearly subscription. Read more in our [Muslim Pro alternative](/blog/en/muslim-pro-alternative/) guide.

## 3. Athan by IslamicFinder: best adhan library

Athan has a large choice of adhan recitations and a cheaper Premium plan. Like Muslim Pro, the adhan is an alert rather than an alarm, and the free version shows ads. See our [Athan app alternative](/blog/en/athan-app-alternative/) comparison.

## 4. Pillars: best minimal times app

Pillars is ad-free, privacy-minded and nicely designed, with good widgets. If you already wake for Fajr without help and want clean times, it is a great choice. It is not built to wake you.

## How to choose

- **You miss Fajr more than you would like:** Salah Alarm.
- **You want one app for everything Islamic:** Muslim Pro.
- **You care most about adhan voices:** Athan.
- **You want quiet, clean prayer times:** Pillars.

Whichever you pick, test it once while you are awake. Set a test adhan five minutes ahead, put the phone on silent, lock it, and see what happens. If you did not hear it, you would not have heard it at Fajr either.
