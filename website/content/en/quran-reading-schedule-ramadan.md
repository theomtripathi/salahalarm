The simplest Quran reading schedule for Ramadan is one juz a day. The standard Madinah mushaf has 604 pages and 30 juz, so reading about 20 pages a day finishes the whole Quran in 30 days. The easiest way to do that is 4 pages after each of the five daily prayers.

Ramadan 2027 is expected to start on Monday 8 February, so the plan below runs from the first night of taraweeh to Eid. If Ramadan is 29 days, you only need to add one extra page here and there.

## The 4 pages after every prayer plan

This plan works because it is tied to something you already do five times a day. You never have to find a long block of free time.

1. **After Fajr:** 4 pages. This is the calmest time of the day, and many people read more here.
2. **After Dhuhr:** 4 pages.
3. **After Asr:** 4 pages.
4. **After Maghrib:** 4 pages, after a light iftar.
5. **After Isha:** 4 pages before taraweeh, or when you get home.

That makes 20 pages, which is one juz. Each sitting takes 6 to 9 minutes for most readers.

The juz number equals the day of Ramadan, which makes it easy to check if you are on track. If you fall behind, add two pages after each prayer for a few days until you catch up.

## Other plans

Not everyone has the same time. Pick the plan that fits your life, and stay with it.

- **Two khatams (40 pages a day):** 8 pages after each prayer. Good for people with more free time, such as students on holiday or retirees.
- **Half the Quran (10 pages a day):** 2 pages after each prayer. A good goal for busy parents or people working long shifts.
- **Last ten nights focus:** read 15 pages a day for the first 20 days, then increase in the last ten nights, when many Muslims seek Laylatul Qadr. Our guide to [Laylatul Qadr 2027](/blog/en/laylatul-qadr-2027/) lists the expected odd nights.
- **Understanding plan:** one page a day with a translation or tafsir, alongside any recitation goal.

## Tips that make the plan stick

**Prepare before Ramadan.** Start reading a few pages a day in the weeks before Ramadan, so the habit is already in place on day one.

**Use the same mushaf.** Page numbers differ between prints. Using one mushaf keeps the schedule exact.

**Read after Fajr when you can.** If you are already up for suhoor and Fajr, this is the best window of the day. A good morning routine starts with [waking for suhoor on time](/blog/en/suhoor-alarm-app/).

**Keep a bookmark or a note.** Write the page you finished after each sitting, so you always know where to continue.

**Do not give up after a missed day.** Missing a day is normal. Spread the missed pages over the next three days instead of trying to read them all at once.

## Reading in the last ten nights

The last ten nights are the most valuable part of Ramadan. Many people plan to finish the Quran on the 27th night, or simply read more in each of these nights. If you have kept to one juz a day, you will start the last ten nights at juz 21 with enough time for extra reading, dua and night prayer.

If you want to pray at night as well, our guide on [how to wake up for tahajjud](/blog/en/how-to-wake-up-for-tahajjud/) explains how to fit it in without losing sleep before Fajr.

## Summary

- 604 pages over 30 days is about 20 pages, or one juz, a day.
- Read 4 pages after each of the five prayers.
- The juz number matches the day of Ramadan.
- Choose two khatams, half the Quran, or one page with tafsir if that fits your life better.
