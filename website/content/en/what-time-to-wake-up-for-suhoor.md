If you have ever asked what time to wake up for suhoor, the honest answer is: it depends on your city, on the day, and on how long your breakfast takes. There is no single time that works for the whole of Ramadan, because imsak moves a little every day. What works is a simple method you can repeat each night.

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin on Monday, 8 February 2027, with the first night of taraweeh on Sunday, 7 February. The method below works for any date.

## Step 1: Find the imsak time for your city

Imsak is the point where the fast begins, a little before Fajr. Your local mosque timetable or a prayer app that calculates times for your location will give you the exact time for each day. Do not rely on one fixed time for the whole month, because it will be wrong by the end.

## Step 2: Count back the time you need

Think about how long you usually take to get up, eat, drink and make wudu. For most people, that is 20 to 40 minutes. If you are cooking, add a little more. Subtract that from the imsak time and you have your wake-up time.

## Step 3: Eat late, but not too late

The sunnah is to delay suhoor until shortly before Fajr. Zayd ibn Thabit radhiyallahu 'anhu reported that the gap between their suhoor with the Prophet ﷺ and the prayer was about the length of fifty verses (Sahih al-Bukhari 1921). This is a good guide: eat calmly, finish before imsak, and do not rush to the point of choking or leaving food behind.

## Step 4: Set two alarms

A single alarm is easy to switch off. Set one alarm for the wake-up time you worked out, and a backup a few minutes after it. Put the phone across the room, so you have to stand up to stop it. Then turn snooze off, because each snooze is a fresh chance to fall back asleep.

## Step 5: Protect the wake-up with an early night

The most common reason people miss suhoor is not the alarm. It is going to bed at 2 am after taraweeh and a long evening. Try to sleep soon after taraweeh, and move screens and visiting to the hours between iftar and Isha. Seven to eight hours of sleep before the alarm is the target.

## Step 6: Do not sleep before Fajr

After you eat, sit up. Read Qur'an, make dhikr or make dua until the adhan for Fajr. Then pray, and rest if you need to. Many people plan a suhoor that works and then lose Fajr because they lie down for "just a moment". The same guidance applies every night.

## A quick plan for the first week

- Check the imsak time for your city each night.
- Count back 30 minutes from imsak and set that as your alarm.
- Keep the phone across the room.
- Aim for bed by the same time every night.
- Pray Fajr each morning, even on the nights you feel tired.

Most people find their body begins waking a few minutes before the alarm after a week or two of this routine. Begin now, while the nights are still long, and the start of Ramadan will feel normal.
