A Fajr alarm app does two jobs that a normal alarm clock cannot. It knows the exact Fajr time where you are, every single day, and it is built to get you out of bed rather than just make a noise. If your current alarm goes off and you still miss Fajr, the problem is almost always the second job.

This guide explains what a good Fajr alarm app should do, how to set one up properly on your iPhone, and the small mistakes that quietly break it.

## What a Fajr alarm app should do

### Calculate Fajr for your exact location

Fajr moves every day with the sun. In London it shifts by well over two hours between winter and summer, and even near the equator it moves by several minutes across the year. A Fajr alarm app uses your location and a calculation method to set the right time automatically, so the alarm is never early, late, or forgotten.

### Play the adhan, not a ringtone

A ringtone tells you it is morning. The adhan tells you why you are getting up. Many people find they respond to it differently, even half asleep.

### Make you get up before it stops

This is the part most prayer apps skip. Their adhan is a notification you can silence with one tap. A real Fajr alarm should only stop once you are out of bed. Salah Alarm does this by asking you to scan your prayer mat with your phone camera. The alarm keeps going until you walk to the mat, so by the time it is quiet you are standing where you pray.

### Keep you from drifting back to bed

Once you are up, the next danger is your phone. SalahLock in Salah Alarm can lock the apps you choose at prayer time until you have prayed, so a "quick look" at messages does not turn into a missed Fajr.

## How to set up a Fajr alarm app on iPhone

1. **Allow location.** Choose "While Using the App" at least, so prayer times match your city.
2. **Pick your calculation method.** Match your local mosque: Muslim World League, Umm al-Qura, ISNA, Egyptian, Diyanet, Kuwait, Qatar, Gulf or Singapore. Choose the Hanafi Asr setting if you follow it.
3. **Allow notifications.** If the app asks for critical alerts, allow them so the alarm can sound through silent mode.
4. **Check Focus modes.** Sleep and Do Not Disturb can block alerts. Add the app to the allowed list.
5. **Set up your prayer mat.** In Salah Alarm, scan your mat once during setup so the app can recognise it at Fajr.
6. **Choose strict, moderate or relaxed.** Strict has no snooze; relaxed allows one snooze before you must scan.
7. **Test it.** Run a test alarm while you are awake and make sure it is loud enough from across the room.

## Mistakes that break your Fajr alarm

- **Phone on the bedside table.** Put it across the room. The walk wakes you up.
- **Low battery.** Plug it in every night. An alarm cannot ring on a dead phone.
- **Sleeping too late.** No alarm can fix five hours of sleep. Count back seven to eight hours from Fajr and treat that as your bedtime.
- **Too many backup alarms.** Five alarms you can swipe away train you to ignore alarms. One alarm you have to get up for works better.
- **Going back to bed "for a minute".** Go straight to wudu. Cold water does more than willpower.

## A Fajr alarm is a tool, not the whole answer

The Prophet ﷺ said that when a person sleeps, Shaytan ties three knots; remembering Allah undoes one, wudu undoes the second, and prayer undoes the last (Sahih al-Bukhari 1142). A good alarm gets you to the first knot. The routine around it does the rest.
