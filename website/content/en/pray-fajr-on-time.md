Praying Fajr on time is hard because it asks you to wake up in the darkest part of the night, when your body wants to sleep most. But it is one of the most rewarding habits you can build. The prayer sets the tone for the whole day, and most people who pray it regularly say they feel calmer, not more tired.

This guide gives you a practical routine: what to do the night before, how to set your alarm, and what to do in the first ten minutes after you wake.

## The night before

Fajr is decided before you go to sleep. Three things help most.

First, fix your bedtime. Count back seven to eight hours from the Fajr time for tomorrow and treat that as the time you go to bed. Pray Isha first, so you are not tempted to delay it.

Second, prepare for the morning. Put your clothes, your prayer mat and a glass of water where you can reach them without turning on the lights. A little preparation saves minutes at the moment you least want to make decisions.

Third, move the phone. If it is next to your bed, you will switch the alarm off half asleep. Put it across the room or outside the door, where you have to get up to reach it.

## Set an alarm that follows Fajr

Fajr shifts by a minute or two most days and by well over an hour across the year in many cities. A fixed alarm time will be early for some weeks and late for others. Choose an alarm that calculates the real Fajr time for your location, every day.

It also helps to use an alarm you cannot stop from your bed. A one-tap notification is easy to dismiss without waking up. An alarm that needs you to stand up and walk is much more reliable.

## The first ten minutes

Most people who miss Fajr do not miss it because they woke up too late. They miss it because they woke up, sat on the bed, and lay back down. Decide the first actions in advance so you do them automatically.

1. **Say the waking dua:** "Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6312).
2. **Turn on a bright light.** Light tells your body that the day has started.
3. **Make wudu with cold water.** Cold water wakes you up faster than anything else.
4. **Go to the mat and pray.** Do not sit down on the way.

The Prophet ﷺ described Shaytan tying three knots on the head of a sleeper. Remembering Allah unties one, wudu unties the second, and prayer unties the third (Sahih al-Bukhari 1142). The steps above follow that picture exactly.

## If you miss Fajr

Missing Fajr happens to everyone. When it does, pray it as soon as you remember, and do not let one miss turn into a week of misses. Then ask one question: why did I miss it? Was it a late night, an alarm I switched off, or a phone within reach? Fix that one cause for the next morning.

## Build the habit gradually

Give yourself two weeks to find a rhythm. In the first few days, you may still need help. After a week or so, many people find their body starts waking a few minutes before the alarm. This is normal. The habit is built by returning, not by never slipping.

It is worth praying Fajr in the mosque when you can. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever prays Isha and Fajr in congregation is as if they prayed the whole night (Sahih Muslim 656). If the mosque is far away, keep praying on time at home, because the reward of consistency is still real.

## A simple checklist

- Fix a bedtime and keep it, even at weekends.
- Pray Isha and go to bed early.
- Set an alarm that follows the real Fajr time.
- Put the phone across the room.
- Say the dua, turn on the light, make wudu, and pray.
