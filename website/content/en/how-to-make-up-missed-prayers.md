Missing a prayer can feel heavy. Many Muslims are not sure whether they must make it up, how to do it, or what to do when they have missed several. The good news is that Islam is merciful, and the path back to prayer is always open. This guide explains the basic guidance and a calm way to rebuild your routine.

Scholars differ on some details, such as the order of multiple missed prayers, so for your own situation it is good to ask a trusted scholar or your local mosque. The steps below cover the basics that most people need.

## The basic guidance

The Prophet ﷺ taught that whoever forgets a prayer, or sleeps through it, should pray it when he remembers it. This hadith is recorded in both Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. It means that a missed prayer should be made up as soon as you become aware of it.

The obligation to pray on time remains, and the mercy of Allah means that the way back is open. Do not let the missed prayer become a reason to give up on the rest of your day.

## Make it up as soon as you remember

When you remember a missed prayer, pray it straight away if you can. If you are in the middle of work, make a short intention to pray it at the first practical moment. Waiting a long time makes it easier to forget, so try to act on it the same day.

If the time for the next prayer is about to begin, you may pray the missed one first, and then pray the current prayer on time. Many people choose to do the missed prayer right after the current one, but the advice is to keep the schedule clear and avoid delaying the current prayer.

## Make up several prayers calmly

If you have missed several prayers, the task can feel overwhelming. Start with the most recent missed prayer, and keep your current prayers on time. Then return to the older ones one at a time. Scholars differ on the exact order and on how many to make up in a day, so ask someone you trust to help you with your situation.

Write a simple list of the prayers you need to make up, and cross each one off when you complete it. Seeing the list shrink helps you stay motivated.

## Keep the current prayers first

A common mistake is to focus so hard on the past that you miss the present prayers. Make the current prayer your first priority. Once you are steady with the current prayers, return to the missed ones gradually.

This approach keeps your worship in balance. The five daily prayers remain the most important part of your day, and the missed prayers are made up alongside them.

## Build a habit that lasts

Missing prayers often happens because the routine is weak. Here are three practical steps that help:

- **Set reminders before each prayer.** A reminder ten minutes early gives you time to prepare.
- **Pair prayer with a daily action.** Pray after a meal, or after a fixed task, so the prayer has a clear place in the day.
- **Track your prayers.** A simple log shows you where the gaps are, so you can plan around them.

## Ask for help

You do not need to rebuild your prayer alone. A friend who prays regularly can remind you, a mosque can give you a timetable, and a trusted scholar can answer specific questions. Asking for help is a sign of seriousness, not weakness.

## What about sleep?

Sleeping through a prayer is common, especially for Fajr. The best protection is a reliable alarm, a regular bedtime, and a phone placed where you cannot reach it from bed. Our guide on [how to wake up for Fajr](/blog/en/how-to-wake-up-for-fajr/) covers these practical steps.

## Do not despair

Some people feel that once they have missed prayers they cannot recover. That feeling is not from Allah. The door of repentance is open, and every step back toward prayer counts. Make sincere repentance, make up the missed prayers as best you can, and continue with the current prayers on time.

## Summary

- Make up a missed prayer as soon as you remember it.
- Keep the current prayers on time, and return to the missed ones gradually.
- Ask a trusted scholar or mosque about the details of your situation.
- Build a steady routine with reminders, daily anchors, and a simple log.
