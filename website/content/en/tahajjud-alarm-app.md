Tahajjud is the night prayer that comes after Isha and before Fajr. It is voluntary, and it is one of the most loved acts of worship for many Muslims, but it is also one of the hardest to wake up for. The night is quiet and the bed is warm, and a single missed alarm can end the plan before it begins.

This guide explains why the last part of the night is special, how to make a sleep plan that makes waking possible, and how to set an alarm you cannot ignore.

## Why the last third of the night matters

The Prophet ﷺ described a moment in the night when Allah draws near to His servants. He said that our Lord descends to the lowest heaven every night when the last third of the night remains, and says: "Who is calling on Me so that I may respond? Who is asking of Me so that I may give him? Who is seeking forgiveness from Me so that I may forgive him?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1145).

That is the reason many people set their alarm for this time. It is not a rule to be strict about. It is an invitation to spend a few quiet minutes in worship when the world is still.

## Start with a sleep plan

You cannot wake for tahajjud on three hours of sleep. Begin by deciding when you will wake, then count backwards. If you want to wake at a certain time, go to bed early enough to get seven or eight hours. Pray Isha, and try to be in bed soon after.

Many people find it helps to sleep early for a few nights first, so the body learns the new rhythm. Do not start by setting a very early alarm and staying up late. That approach usually fails within a week.

## Set an alarm you cannot ignore

A gentle alarm is easy to switch off without waking. Choose a sound that is clear and loud enough to wake you, and set it for a time you have chosen in advance.

Then make the alarm stop only when you have stood up. Put the phone across the room, so you have to leave the bed to reach it. Turn off snooze, because the second alarm is where most people fall back asleep.

Salah Alarm can help with the prayer side of this. It calculates the time for Fajr each day, and it plays the adhan as an alarm that stops only when you scan your prayer mat with your phone camera. That keeps you on your feet for the Fajr that follows the night prayer. For tahajjud itself, a plain alarm set for the time you choose works just as well.

## The first minutes after you wake

Once the alarm goes off, follow a short routine before you think about prayer.

1. Say the waking dua: "Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6312).
2. Turn on a light.
3. Make wudu with cold water.
4. Go to your place of prayer and begin.

The Prophet ﷺ described knots that Shaytan ties on a sleeper, and how remembering Allah, making wudu and praying each untie one (Sahih al-Bukhari 1142). That picture fits tahajjud perfectly: the dhikr, the wudu and the prayer are how you begin the night's worship.

## Start small and keep it

You do not need a long prayer to begin. Two or four rak'ahs, prayed slowly and with attention, are a good start. Once that becomes a habit, you can add more when you feel ready. Consistency matters more than length. A small prayer kept every week is better than a long one attempted once.

If you miss a night, do not give up. Return the next night, and keep your sleep plan steady. Many people find that their body starts waking a few minutes before the alarm after a couple of weeks.

## A simple plan for the first two weeks

- **Week one:** go to bed earlier, set your alarm for the time you have chosen, and keep the phone across the room.
- **Week two:** pray two or four rak'ahs when you wake, and add a few minutes of dua at the end.
