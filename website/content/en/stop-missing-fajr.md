To stop missing Fajr, work out why you miss it, and fix that reason. Generic advice like "sleep early" and "make dua" is true, but if your real problem is an alarm you switch off half asleep, sleeping an hour earlier will not save you. Most missed Fajrs come from one of six causes. Find yours below.

## 1. You go to bed too late

**The sign:** you hear the alarm, but you are exhausted and it feels impossible to move.

**The fix:** count back seven to eight hours from Fajr, and treat that as your bedtime, not a suggestion. Pray Isha, put the phone on charge away from the bed, and stop screens 30 minutes before. If that is a big jump, move your bedtime 15 minutes earlier every few nights. The Prophet ﷺ disliked sleeping before Isha and talking after it (Sahih al-Bukhari 568), and that guidance protects Fajr.

## 2. Your alarm is too easy to switch off

**The sign:** you wake up at sunrise, the alarm went off, and you do not remember touching it.

**The fix:** use an alarm you cannot stop from your bed. In the first minute after waking your brain runs on autopilot, and a one-tap button gets pressed without you deciding anything. An alarm that needs you to stand up, walk and do something with your hands breaks that autopilot.

## 3. Your phone is within reach

**The sign:** you snooze three times, each one "just five minutes".

**The fix:** put the phone across the room, or outside the door if you can still hear it. Turn snooze off. Each snooze is a fresh chance to fall back asleep.

## 4. Fajr moves, your alarm does not

**The sign:** you were fine in summer and started missing it in autumn, or the other way round.

**The fix:** Fajr shifts by a minute or two most days and by well over an hour across the year in many cities. A fixed 5:00 alarm will be early for months and late for months. Use an alarm that follows the actual Fajr time for where you are.

## 5. You have no plan for the first minute

**The sign:** you wake up, sit on the bed and lie back down "for a second".

**The fix:** decide the first three actions in advance and do them without thinking. Say the waking dua, "Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6312). Switch on a bright light. Walk to the sink and make wudu with cold water. The Prophet ﷺ described three knots Shaytan ties on a sleeper: remembering Allah unties one, wudu the second, and prayer the third (Sahih al-Bukhari 1142).

## 6. You give up after a bad week

**The sign:** you missed three in a row, felt bad, and stopped trying.

**The fix:** expect misses, and plan for them. When you miss one, pray it as soon as you wake, then ask one question: which of the five causes above was it? Fix that one cause for the next night. A habit is built by returning, not by never slipping.

## Why it is worth the fight

The Prophet ﷺ said the two rak'ahs before Fajr are better than this world and everything in it (Sahih Muslim 725), and that whoever prays Isha and Fajr in congregation is as if they prayed the whole night (Sahih Muslim 656). Fajr is hard on purpose, and it is the prayer that sets the tone for the whole day.

## A 7-day plan

1. **Night 1:** set your bedtime and move the phone across the room.
2. **Night 2:** switch to an alarm that follows Fajr and cannot be stopped from bed.
3. **Night 3:** write your first-minute plan on a note by the door.
4. **Nights 4-7:** keep the same bedtime, even on the weekend.
