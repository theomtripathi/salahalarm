Most of us know the moment. The adhan goes off, and you tell yourself you will pray in a minute, after one quick message or one more video. A minute later the prayer time has half gone. An app that locks your phone until you pray tries to fix that moment, by putting a clear pause between you and the screen.

This guide explains how these locks work, what to look for, and how to use one without getting stuck.

## What a prayer lock does

A prayer lock blocks the apps that usually pull you away during prayer time. You choose which apps are blocked, and the lock stays on until you complete the prayer. The idea is simple: the phone is still there, but it does not compete with the prayer.

This is different from a reminder. A reminder tells you the time. A lock makes the distraction harder to reach. For many people, the reminder is ignored and the lock is what gets them to stand up.

## What to check before you rely on one

1. **Which apps it blocks.** Make sure you can choose the apps that distract you most, such as social media and messaging.
2. **How it releases.** A good lock releases when you confirm the prayer, so you are not stuck on the screen.
3. **Emergency access.** A prayer lock should never stop emergency calls. Test this once, while you are awake.
4. **Prayer times.** The lock is only useful if it starts at the right time for your city. Check that it uses a calculation method your mosque also uses.
5. **Privacy.** It needs your location for prayer times, but it should not use that location to follow you in other apps.

## Setting it up without getting stuck

1. **Start with two or three apps.** Pick the ones that take the most of your time, and leave the rest alone.
2. **Test the release.** Pray once while the lock is on, and confirm it releases as you expect.
3. **Test an emergency call.** Make sure you can reach emergency services and your important contacts while the lock is active.
4. **Check the times.** Look at today's prayer times in the app and compare them with your mosque's timetable.

## Use it as a habit, not a trap

A lock works best as a gentle reminder that the prayer comes first. It does not replace the habit. If you find yourself avoiding prayer to get back to your phone, that is a signal to change the time you pray, or to choose fewer apps to block.

Many people find that after a few weeks, they stop reaching for the phone at prayer time at all. The lock is then a backup, not the main method.

## A simple first week

- **Day 1:** choose two apps to block, and set the lock to your local prayer times.
- **Days 2 to 4:** pray with the lock on, and note any prayer where you felt tempted to skip it.
- **Days 5 to 7:** keep the same settings and add a third app only if the first two are working.
