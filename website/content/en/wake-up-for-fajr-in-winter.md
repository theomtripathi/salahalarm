Winter is when Fajr feels hardest. The nights are long, the air is cold, and the bed is warm. Many people who pray Fajr easily in summer find that the first cold mornings of autumn and winter are a real struggle. The good news is that winter Fajr is manageable with a few changes to your routine.

This guide explains why winter Fajr is harder and gives you a plan for the darkest mornings.

## Why winter Fajr is harder

In winter, the nights are long and the mornings are dark for longer. Your body is more inclined to stay in bed when it is dark and cold, because it reads the darkness as "still night". The warmth of the duvet is also harder to leave than in summer.

Winter also brings practical problems. Heating goes on at night, bedrooms feel stuffy, and the first step out of bed is into cold air. All of this makes getting up harder, even when you want to.

## Check the Fajr time for your city

Fajr does not stay in one place through the year. In many cities it comes later in winter than in summer, and it changes a little each day. A fixed alarm that worked in October may be wrong by December. Use a timetable or an app that calculates Fajr for your location every day, so your alarm always matches the real time.

If you use Salah Alarm, it calculates Fajr daily for where you are and plays the adhan as an alarm. You can choose the calculation method your mosque uses, and set strict mode so there is no snooze.

## Keep the bedroom cool at night, warm when you wake

A very warm bedroom makes sleep heavy, and heavy sleep is harder to break. Keep the room cool at night. Then, before you leave the bed, warm up the room, so the first minutes of the morning are not a shock.

You can also use a heated room or a warm drink, if that helps you stand up. Keep it simple: the aim is to make getting up as easy as possible, not to make the room comfortable enough to stay in bed.

## Use light

Light is one of the most effective tools for waking up. As soon as your alarm goes off, turn on the brightest light in the room. Light tells your body that the day has started, and it stops the sleepy feeling faster than almost anything else.

If you can, use a light that comes on gradually before the alarm. Even a lamp switched on a few minutes early can help.

## Plan the first five minutes

In winter, the first minutes are the most dangerous. You feel cold, you want to pull the blanket back up, and it is easy to stay where you are. Decide what happens in those minutes before you go to bed.

1. Stand up and turn on the light.
2. Say the waking dua: "Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6312).
3. Walk to the sink and make wudu, even though the water is cold.
4. Go straight to the mat and pray.

Keeping your prayer mat and clothes by the bed, and your phone across the room, makes this much easier.

## Sleep earlier

Long nights are tempting. You may feel you have time to watch one more video or talk a little longer. Set an earlier bedtime for winter, and keep it. Seven to eight hours of sleep before Fajr is the target, and it matters more in winter than in summer.

Try to pray Isha and go to bed soon after. If you have trouble falling asleep, reduce screens for half an hour before bed, and keep your room quiet and dark.

## Build the habit before the coldest weeks

Do not wait until the middle of winter to start. Begin now, while the mornings are still easy, and build the routine gradually. By the time it gets colder, waking up for Fajr will feel more natural.

## Summary

- Set your alarm to the real Fajr time for your city each day.
- Keep your bedroom cool at night and warm when you wake.
- Turn on the light as soon as you get up.
- Have a first-five-minutes routine and follow it.
- Go to bed early enough to get seven to eight hours of sleep.
