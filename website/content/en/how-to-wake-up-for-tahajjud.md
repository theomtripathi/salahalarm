Tahajjud is the night prayer that many Muslims want to make a habit. It is quiet, personal, and close to the heart, and many people feel drawn to it during Ramadan or in the last part of the night. Yet waking up in the dark is hard, and most people who try give up after a few nights.

This guide explains when Tahajjud is prayed, a gentle plan for waking up, and how to make the habit last.

## When is Tahajjud prayed?

Tahajjud is an optional night prayer. It is prayed after the Isha prayer and before Fajr. The Prophet ﷺ encouraged his companions to pray at night, and he described Allah's descent to the lowest heaven in the last part of the night, when He says that whoever calls upon Him will be answered (Sahih al-Bukhari).

Many people find that the last third of the night is the best time, because it is calm and quiet. However, you do not need to wait for that time. Praying in any part of the night is a good act, and it is better to pray a little regularly than a lot once.

## Start with a realistic goal

The biggest mistake is to begin with a large goal, such as waking up every night for a long prayer. A better start is to choose a small goal, such as two rak'ahs, three nights a week. The Prophet ﷺ taught that night prayer is prayed in pairs. You can keep it short and steady, and increase it slowly over several weeks.

## Protect your sleep first

Tahajjud is easier when you sleep well. Plan a regular bedtime, and try to fall asleep at the same hour most nights. A short nap in the afternoon can help if you are not sleeping well at night. The aim is to wake up feeling rested enough to pray, rather than sleep-deprived.

## Use a gentle alarm

Choose an alarm that wakes you without shocking you. A soft tone that gradually increases works well for many people. Place the phone across the room, so you have to stand up to turn it off. Then make wudu and begin.

If you already use a Fajr alarm, you may set a second one for the night prayer. Keep the alarms at a time you can keep, and test them for a few nights before you rely on them.

## Make wudu and start small

Make wudu as soon as you stand up. Wudu wakes the body and sets the mind on prayer. Begin with the two rak'ahs you planned. If you feel sleepy, keep the recitation short and focus on the meaning of what you read.

Then make dua. The night is a special time for asking Allah, and many people use the last moments of the prayer to ask for guidance, forgiveness and strength for the day.

## Keep the habit steady

Once you have woken for a week, add a little more time. Keep the prayer at a level that feels sustainable, and avoid comparing yourself to others. The Prophet ﷺ said that the most beloved deeds to Allah are the ones done regularly, even if they are small.

If you miss a night, do not punish yourself. Return to your routine the next night. A missed night is not the end of the habit.

## What about missing Fajr?

Tahajjud should never cause you to miss Fajr. If you are not sure whether you will wake in time, pray Fajr first and leave Tahajjud for another night. The obligatory prayers come before optional ones, and a steady routine protects both.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting too ambitious.** Choose a small goal you can keep.
- **Skipping sleep to pray.** Protect your sleep, so the prayer is not an act of exhaustion.
- **Relying on one alarm.** Set a backup, and test your setup.
- **Giving up after one missed night.** Return the next night and keep going.

## Summary

- Tahajjud is an optional night prayer, prayed after Isha and before Fajr.
- Start with a small goal and increase it slowly.
- Protect your sleep and use a gentle alarm placed across the room.
- Never let Tahajjud cause you to miss Fajr.
