Most people do not struggle with salah because they do not care about it. They struggle because life is busy, the times change every day, and a prayer missed once quickly becomes two, then five. Consistency is not a personality trait. It is a set of small habits that make the next prayer easier to pray than to skip.

This guide gives you seven habits that work for busy people, students, parents and shift workers alike.

## 1. Start with one prayer

Choose the prayer you miss most often. For many people it is Fajr, for others it is Asr, when the day is full and the time slips past. Commit to that one prayer for two weeks before you add another. A single prayer kept on time is worth more than five prayers kept sometimes.

## 2. Know the exact time, every day

Prayer times move through the year, and a fixed mental picture of them will drift out of date. Check the time for your city each morning, or use a prayer app that calculates it for your location. Knowing the exact time removes the small guesswork that makes people delay.

## 3. Set a reminder before the time, not at it

A reminder that arrives at the exact prayer time catches you in the middle of something. A reminder ten minutes earlier gives you time to finish, make wudu and walk to your place of prayer. Set it for each prayer you are working on.

## 4. Tie the prayer to a habit you already have

Attach the prayer to something you do every day. Pray Dhuhr after you park the car, or pray Maghrib before you sit down to iftar. The habit you already have becomes the reminder, and you do not need to remember anything extra.

## 5. Make a place for it

Keep a clean, consistent spot for prayer at home or work, and keep your mat there. When the place is ready, the prayer starts sooner. This is especially useful at work, where a quiet corner, or even a clear space by your desk, makes a real difference.

## 6. Protect the hardest prayer with sleep

If Fajr is the one you miss, the fix is usually the night before. Set a bedtime that gives you seven or eight hours before the prayer, put your phone out of reach, and prepare your mat and clothes. Consistent Fajr starts with consistent sleep.

Salah Alarm can help with this part. It calculates each prayer time for your location every day, and it plays the adhan for Fajr as an alarm that stops only when you scan your prayer mat with your phone camera. Its strict mode has no snooze, so you have to get up to stop it.

## 7. Track it for a week

Write down each prayer you pray on time, every day, for one week. The paper or the notes app is enough. Seeing the record fill in encourages you, and gaps show you which prayers and which hours are hardest. Plan around those gaps in the week that follows, instead of guessing.

## When you miss one

Everyone misses prayers. What matters is what you do next. Pray the missed prayer as soon as you remember, do not treat it as a reason to give up, and ask one question: what caused it? Then fix that cause for the next prayer. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever forgets a prayer should pray it when they remember (Sahih Muslim 684). Returning quickly is part of being consistent.

## A simple two-week plan

- **Week one:** choose one prayer, set a reminder before it, and track it every day.
- **Week two:** keep that prayer, tie it to a daily habit, and add a second prayer only if the first is steady.
