Ramadan 2027 is expected to run from **Monday 8 February to Monday 8 or Tuesday 9 March 2027**, with **Eid al-Fitr on Tuesday 9 or Wednesday 10 March**. Below is the full Ramadan 2027 calendar day by day, the dates of the last ten nights and the odd nights, and how to get exact suhoor and iftar times for your city.

These dates are based on astronomical forecasts. Turkey's Diyanet has already published the same start date and a 29-day month. Most other countries confirm the month by moon sighting, so your local start can move by a day. If it does, shift every date below by one day.

## Key dates at a glance

- **First taraweeh:** evening of Sunday 7 February 2027
- **First fast (1 Ramadan):** Monday 8 February 2027
- **Middle of Ramadan (15 Ramadan):** Monday 22 February 2027
- **Last ten nights begin:** evening of Saturday 27 February 2027
- **27th night:** evening of Friday 5 March 2027
- **Expected Eid al-Fitr:** Tuesday 9 March or Wednesday 10 March 2027

Remember that Islamic days begin at sunset. The "night" of a Ramadan day is the evening before its fast. That is why the first taraweeh is on Sunday evening and the first fast is on Monday.

## The last ten nights and Laylat al-Qadr

The Prophet ﷺ told us to seek Laylat al-Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten (Sahih al-Bukhari 2017). With a start on 8 February, those odd nights are:

1. **21st night:** evening of Saturday 27 February
2. **23rd night:** evening of Monday 1 March
3. **25th night:** evening of Wednesday 3 March
4. **27th night:** evening of Friday 5 March
5. **29th night:** evening of Sunday 7 March

Many people put extra effort into the 27th night, but no single night is certain. The safest plan is to give all ten nights your best effort: extra prayer, Quran, dua and charity.

## Suhoor and iftar times

The calendar above gives the dates. The times depend on where you live. Suhoor ends at imsak, just before Fajr, and iftar is at Maghrib (sunset). Both shift by a minute or so each day.

Because Ramadan 2027 falls in February, fasts are relatively short for most of the world: roughly 11 to 13 hours in the UK, Europe, North America, the Gulf and South Asia. The hard part is not the length of the fast but waking up for suhoor in the dark.
