The best Fajr alarm app is the one you cannot beat in your sleep. Most prayer apps get the time right but play the adhan as a notification: one tap and it is gone, and so is Fajr. A good Fajr alarm needs two things: the exact Fajr time for where you are, every day, and a way to make sure you are actually up before it goes quiet.

We compared the five apps people most often use for Fajr against those two tests. App Store details were checked on 26 October 2026.

## The quick answer

| | Exact Fajr time daily | Must get up to stop it | Ads in free version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salah Alarm | Yes | Yes, prayer mat scan | No |
| Muslim Pro | Yes | Not in its listing | Yes |
| Athan (IslamicFinder) | Yes | Not in its listing | Yes |
| Pillars | Yes | Not offered | No |
| Alarmy | No, set by hand | Yes, missions | Yes |

Only two apps pass the "must get up" test, and only one of them knows when Fajr is.

## 1. Salah Alarm: best for actually waking up

Full disclosure: this is our app. We built it because every other option failed one of the two tests.

Salah Alarm works out the exact Fajr time for your location with the major calculation methods (Muslim World League, Umm al-Qura, ISNA, Diyanet and others) and plays the adhan as an alarm. It stops only when you scan your prayer mat with your phone camera, so by the time it is quiet you are standing where you pray. You can choose strict mode with no snooze, or a relaxed mode that allows one snooze. SalahLock can also lock distracting apps until you have prayed. No ads.

**Falls short if:** you want a big content app with community features and lectures. Salah Alarm is focused on salah.

## 2. Muslim Pro: best all-in-one, weakest at Fajr

Muslim Pro does everything: prayer times, Quran, Qibla, community and more, with over 600,000 ratings in the US. But the adhan is an alert you can tap away, the free version has ads (Premium is $12.99 a month or $34.99 a year in the US), and its privacy label lists location and identifiers as used to track you. More in our [Muslim Pro alternative](/blog/en/muslim-pro-alternative/) guide.

## 3. Athan by IslamicFinder: best adhan voices

Athan has a great library of adhan recitations and cheaper Premium ($1.99 a month or $9.99 a year in the US). Same Fajr problem, though: it is an alert, not an alarm, and the free version shows ads. See our [Athan app alternative](/blog/en/athan-app-alternative/) guide.

## 4. Pillars: best clean prayer-times app

Pillars is ad-free, privacy-minded and beautifully designed. If you are already a reliable Fajr riser and just want accurate times and widgets, it is excellent. It does not try to be an alarm.

## 5. Alarmy: the toughest alarm, but it does not know Fajr

Alarmy is a general alarm clock famous for "missions": you have to take a photo, shake the phone or solve maths problems to stop it. It works for getting up. The catch is that it has no idea when Fajr is. Fajr moves by a minute or two most days and by well over an hour across the year in many cities, so you end up resetting it by hand, and the first morning you forget is the morning you miss it.

## How we would choose

1. **You keep missing Fajr:** Salah Alarm. Prayer times and a must-get-up alarm in one.
2. **You already wake up fine:** Pillars for clean times, or Muslim Pro if you want everything.
3. **You want an alarm for everything, not just Fajr:** Alarmy, but update it as Fajr moves.

An app is half of it. The other half is sleep: go to bed seven to eight hours before Fajr and keep the phone across the room. Our guide on [how to wake up for Fajr](/blog/en/how-to-wake-up-for-fajr/) has the full routine.
