The first moment after waking up is a quiet time. The mind is still soft, the body is still heavy, and the day has not yet started. The Prophet ﷺ gave us words for that moment, so that the first thing we say when we open our eyes is a remembrance of Allah.

This guide explains the dua after waking up, when to say it, and a simple way to make it a habit.

## The dua after waking up

The best known dua is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari (6312). The Prophet ﷺ said that when he woke up he would say:

"الحمد لله الذي أحيانا بعد ما أماتنا وإليه النشور"

In English, this means: "Praise is to Allah who gave us life after He had caused us to die, and to Him is the resurrection."

The dua is short and easy to learn. It reminds you that sleep is a kind of death, and that waking is a gift from Allah. It also reminds you that everything will return to Him, which helps you start the day with the right intention.

## When to say it

The usual practice is to say it as soon as you wake up. Some people say it while they are still lying down, and others say it after they sit up. Both are fine. The point is to make it the first thing you say, before you pick up your phone or start thinking about tasks.

If you wake up for the Fajr alarm, say the dua as the alarm stops, then make wudu and pray. This keeps the order simple: remembrance first, then the prayer.

## A longer dua for waking at night

The Prophet ﷺ also taught a longer dua for those who wake during the night. In a hadith recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari, he said that whoever wakes at night and says a particular dua, and then asks for something, will have his prayer accepted. The dua begins with praise of Allah, affirms that there is no god but Him, and asks for forgiveness and guidance.

Many people say the short dua when they wake for the day, and the longer one when they wake for night prayer. If you find the longer one hard to memorise, start with the short one and add the longer one once it feels familiar.

## Make it a habit

A dua is easiest to keep when it is attached to something you already do. Here are three simple ways:

- **Keep the words near your bed.** Write the dua on a card and place it beside your alarm or your water bottle.
- **Say it as the alarm stops.** When the alarm goes off, say the dua before you do anything else.
- **Link it to sitting up.** Make it the first thing you say when your feet touch the floor.

Pick one method and use it for a week. Once it feels natural, you can try another.

## Common questions

**Does it matter if I say it in Arabic or in my own language?** The words of the dua are best said in Arabic, because that is how the Prophet ﷺ taught it. If you do not yet know the Arabic, learn it slowly, and in the meantime say the meaning in your own language with sincerity.

**What if I wake up late?** Say it when you wake, whatever the time. It is never too late for remembrance.

**Can I say it while I am still half asleep?** Say it as clearly as you can once you are awake. The aim is to start the day with the remembrance of Allah, not to rush through the words.

## Pair it with Fajr

For many Muslims, the moment after waking is the start of Fajr. If you use an alarm for Fajr, keep the dua in the same routine: alarm, dua, wudu, prayer. Each step is short, and together they make the morning calm. If you struggle to wake on time, read [how to wake up for Fajr](/blog/en/how-to-wake-up-for-fajr/) and [pray Fajr on time](/blog/en/pray-fajr-on-time/).

Salah Alarm sets the Fajr adhan as an alarm, so the adhan is the first sound you hear. Saying the dua as the adhan stops makes the start of the day both practical and meaningful.

## A short morning routine

1. Say the dua as soon as you wake.
2. Sit up and take a moment to remember Allah.
3. Make wudu.
4. Pray Fajr on time.
5. Read a little Qur'an, if you can.

This routine takes only a few minutes, and it turns waking up into worship. Start with the dua, and build the rest slowly.
