One of the first questions people ask about Ramadan is how long the fast will be. The answer depends on where you live and on the day of the month. In Ramadan 2027, which is expected to begin on 8 February, the fast will change from day to day, and it will be different in every city.

This guide explains how fasting hours are measured, how they change through the month, and how to plan your suhoor and iftar around them.

## How the fast is measured

The fast begins at the start of Fajr, and it ends at sunset, when Maghrib begins. The length of the fast is the time between those two points. Fajr is a little before sunrise, so the fast is slightly longer than the daylight hours alone.

Because the times depend on the position of the sun, they vary with your latitude and with the date. Two cities on the same day can have very different fasting hours.

## How the hours change through the month

In the northern hemisphere, the days are getting longer through February and into March. That means the fast becomes longer as Ramadan goes on. People who begin the month with a shorter fast often find that the last week feels longer, even if they are used to the routine.

In the southern hemisphere, the pattern is the reverse. The days are shortening, so the fast becomes a little shorter over the same weeks.

## How to check your own times

Do not rely on a single number from the internet. Use these steps instead:

1. Find the official timetable for your country, or a prayer app that calculates times for your city.
2. Check the Fajr and Maghrib times for at least the first day, and then check them again each week.
3. Use the same method your local mosque uses, if you can, so your times match the community.
4. Keep a note of your suhoor and iftar times, and set your alarms from them.

## Plan suhoor around the fast

Suhoor should end at Fajr, so the time to eat is the last part of the night. Many people find that a small, balanced meal helps them through a longer fast. Protein, fibre and water are useful choices, and a large heavy meal often makes the day harder.

Our guide on [what time to wake up for suhoor](/blog/en/what-time-to-wake-up-for-suhoor/) explains how to plan the wake-up time so you do not rush your meal.

## Plan iftar and the evening

Iftar is the moment of sunset. Break your fast with water and something light, then pray Maghrib. Once you have eaten, you can plan taraweeh and the rest of the evening. A short rest after iftar can help you through the night, especially in the second half of the month.

## Adjust your daily routine

As the days change, adjust your routine gently. If the fast becomes longer, plan your work and your reading for the morning, when your energy is higher. If the fast is shorter, you can keep the same routine with less effort.

Remember that the goal is steady worship, not a perfect schedule. If you feel tired, rest when you can, and keep the obligatory prayers on time.

## Common mistakes

- **Using one set of times for the whole month.** The times change, so check them regularly.
- **Eating too late before Fajr.** Stop your suhoor in time, and do not count on a minute or two.
- **Ignoring sleep.** A short fast is easier to keep when you have slept well, and the same is true of a long one.

## Summary

- Fasting hours depend on your city and the date.
- The fast runs from Fajr to sunset, and the length changes through the month.
- Check your times regularly, using your country's timetable or a reliable prayer app.
- Plan suhoor and iftar around the times, and protect your sleep.
