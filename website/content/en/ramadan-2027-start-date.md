Ramadan 2027 is closer than it feels. Based on astronomical calculations, the month of Ramadan 1448 AH is expected to begin on **Monday 8 February 2027**, which means the first night of taraweeh would be on the evening of Sunday 7 February.

Like every year, the exact date is confirmed only when the crescent moon is sighted or when each country's religious authority makes its announcement. Below are the expected key dates, why they can shift by a day, and how to use the next few months to prepare.

## Expected Ramadan 2027 dates at a glance

- **First taraweeh:** evening of Sunday 7 February 2027
- **First fast:** Monday 8 February 2027
- **Last ten nights begin:** around the evening of Saturday 27 February 2027
- **Expected Eid al-Fitr:** Tuesday 9 March or Wednesday 10 March 2027

These dates come from astronomical forecasts for the Gulf region, including those reported by astronomers in the UAE. Your local mosque or national authority may announce a date one day earlier or later.

## How the start of Ramadan is decided

The Islamic calendar is lunar. Each month begins when the new crescent (hilal) is seen after sunset on the 29th of the previous month. If the crescent is not seen, the month completes 30 days. Because a lunar year is about 11 days shorter than the solar year, Ramadan moves earlier each year: Ramadan 2026 began around 18 February, and Ramadan 2027 arrives about ten or eleven days sooner.

Different communities use different methods:

- **Local sighting:** the month starts when the moon is seen in that country, as in Pakistan and many communities in South Asia.
- **Following Saudi Arabia:** many communities in Europe and the Gulf follow the announcement from Saudi Arabia.
- **Astronomical calculation:** Turkey and some organisations in North America fix dates in advance using calculations.

That is why your cousin in Karachi and your friend in London may start fasting on different days. Follow your local mosque or the method your community uses.

## What a February Ramadan means for your fasts

A February Ramadan is one of the gentler ones for most of the Northern Hemisphere. Days are shorter, so fasts in the UK, Europe and North America are around 11 to 13 hours, and cooler weather makes them easier. In the Gulf, Pakistan and Indonesia, fasting hours are also moderate, roughly 12 to 13 hours.

The trade-off is that suhoor still falls in the early morning, which is exactly when most people find it hardest to wake up. Getting suhoor right is what makes or breaks the month.

## How to prepare for Ramadan 2027 starting now

Three months is enough time to build the habits that make Ramadan easier:

1. **Fix your Fajr first.** If you can wake up for Fajr every day now, waking up for suhoor in February will feel normal instead of painful.
2. **Make up missed fasts.** If you owe fasts from last Ramadan, plan them before the month begins. Short winter days make this easier.
3. **Build a Quran routine.** Start with one page a day after Fajr, and grow it so you can aim to finish the Quran in Ramadan.
4. **Fast some Mondays and Thursdays.** Voluntary fasts in Rajab and Sha'ban prepare your body for the month.
5. **Plan your goals.** Write down what you want from this Ramadan: prayers, Quran, charity and one bad habit to drop.
