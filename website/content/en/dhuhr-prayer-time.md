Dhuhr is the midday prayer, and for many people it falls in the middle of a busy workday. It is easy to forget, to push back, or to pray late without noticing. Knowing exactly when Dhuhr begins and ends, and how those times are set, makes it easier to pray it on time.

This guide explains how the Dhuhr time is found, why the times can differ slightly between apps and timetables, and how to build a simple habit around it.

## How the Dhuhr time is set

Dhuhr begins when the sun has passed its highest point in the sky and begins to decline toward the west. That moment is called the zenith. In practice it is shortly after noon by the clock, but the exact time changes with your longitude, the date, and the time zone you are in.

Because the sun's position changes through the year, the Dhuhr time moves by a few minutes most days. This is why a fixed time copied from one day to the next is not reliable, and why a daily calculation is useful.

## When Dhuhr ends

Dhuhr continues until the time for Asr begins. The Asr time is set by the length of an object's shadow, and scholars differ on the exact shadow length used. The Hanafi school uses a longer shadow than the other schools, so the Asr time, and with it the end of Dhuhr, can be a little later in that method.

This is the main reason for differences between timetables. If you follow a particular mosque, use the same method they do, so that your Dhuhr and Asr times match theirs.

## Why apps can disagree

Calculation methods differ in the angle used for Fajr and Isha, and in the shadow rule used for Asr. Dhuhr itself is usually the most stable of the five times, but the boundary between Dhuhr and Asr depends on the method. An app that offers several methods lets you choose the one that fits your community, which is more useful than trusting a single number.

## How to pray Dhuhr on time

Many people miss Dhuhr because they are busy, not because they forget. A few simple steps help:

1. **Set a reminder before the time.** A reminder ten minutes early gives you time to finish what you are doing.
2. **Choose a clean place to pray.** Keep a prayer mat or a quiet corner at work or at home.
3. **Make wudu in advance.** When the time comes, you only need to pray.
4. **Pray as soon as it begins, when you can.** Praying early is good, and it removes the risk of forgetting.

If you cannot pray at the start, pray before the time for Asr begins. Never leave Dhuhr until the last minutes if you can avoid it.

## Dhuhr and the rest of the day

Dhuhr often sets the rhythm of the afternoon. When you pray it on time, the rest of the day has a clear shape: Asr, then Maghrib, then Isha. Many people find that the first prayer of the afternoon makes it easier to keep the others.

If your work does not allow a break, look for a short time in the day that you can use. A few minutes is enough for wudu and a prayer, and a steady habit is worth more than a long break you cannot repeat.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying a fixed time.** The Dhuhr time changes through the year, so check it regularly.
- **Mixing methods.** Choose one method and keep to it, so your Dhuhr and Asr boundaries are consistent.
- **Delaying until the last minute.** Pray as soon as you can, and do not wait for the end of the time.

## Summary

- Dhuhr begins when the sun passes its zenith and declines.
- It ends when the time for Asr begins, which depends on the Asr method used.
- Times can differ between apps, so use the method your mosque follows.
- Set a reminder, prepare wudu, and pray as soon as you can.
