A fast in Ramadan is broken by eating, drinking, or sexual intercourse on purpose between Fajr and Maghrib, by making yourself vomit, and by the start of menstruation or post-natal bleeding. Eating or drinking by mistake, because you forgot you were fasting, does not break it.

That covers most questions. The rest of this guide goes through each point calmly, lists the things that do not break the fast, and explains when a missed day is made up. Scholars differ on some details, especially modern medical treatments, so for your own situation it is always good to ask a trusted local scholar.

## When the fast begins and ends

The fast starts at true dawn, which is the start of Fajr, and ends at sunset, which is the start of Maghrib. Many timetables show an imsak time a few minutes before Fajr. Imsak is a caution margin to stop eating, not the actual start of the fast.

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin on Monday 8 February. Fajr and Maghrib change every day, so check a timetable for your city. Our [Ramadan 2027 timetables by city](/ramadan-timetable-2027/) list sehri and iftar times for every day of the month.

## What breaks your fast

These are the main things that invalidate a Ramadan fast when they happen knowingly and by choice:

1. **Eating or drinking on purpose.** Any food or drink that reaches the stomach through the mouth or nose. This includes water, juice, gum that releases sugar, and smoking.
2. **Sexual intercourse during the day.** This breaks the fast and has its own serious ruling of expiation (kaffarah). Ask a scholar if this applies to you.
3. **Making yourself vomit.** Vomiting on purpose breaks the fast, as reported in Sunan Abu Dawud and Jami at-Tirmidhi.
4. **Menstruation or post-natal bleeding.** If bleeding starts, even a few minutes before Maghrib, the fast of that day is not valid and is made up later.
5. **Intending to break the fast.** Many scholars say a firm decision to stop fasting ends the fast, even before eating.

## What does not break your fast

Many people worry about things that are fine. These do not break the fast:

- **Eating or drinking by mistake.** The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever forgets and eats or drinks should complete the fast, because Allah fed him and gave him drink (Sahih al-Bukhari 1933, Sahih Muslim 1155).
- **Vomiting you cannot control.** If it happens on its own, the fast is still valid.
- **Brushing teeth or using a miswak,** as long as you do not swallow.
- **Rinsing the mouth and nose in wudu,** without going too far so water goes down the throat.
- **Swallowing your own saliva.**
- **Showering, swimming carefully, or pouring water over yourself** to cool down.
- **Tasting food with the tongue** while cooking, if you spit it out and nothing is swallowed. Many scholars allow this when there is a need.
- **Waking up in a state of major impurity (janabah).** Make ghusl and continue fasting.
- **Blood tests.** Most scholars say a small blood test does not break the fast.

## Medical treatments

Questions about inhalers, eye drops, injections and drips are common. Scholars differ on several of them. A general guide many scholars give is that nutrition entering the body, such as a nutritional drip, breaks the fast, while non-nutritional injections do not. Inhalers and drops are debated.

Because the answer depends on the exact treatment, ask a trusted scholar, and speak to your doctor before Ramadan if you take regular medicine. People who are ill, travelling, pregnant, breastfeeding or elderly have concessions in Islam, so you may not need to fast at all.

## If you broke a fast: making it up

When a fast is broken for a valid reason, such as illness, travel or menstruation, the day is made up after Ramadan. You fast one day for each day missed, before the next Ramadan if you can.

If a fast was broken on purpose without a valid reason, sincere repentance is needed as well as making up the day. Intercourse during a Ramadan fast has the extra ruling of kaffarah, which is serious, so ask a scholar directly.

Keep a simple note of the days you owe. Our guide to [how to make up missed prayers](/blog/en/how-to-make-up-missed-prayers/) suggests a list you cross off, and the same idea works well for missed fasts.

## Common mistakes

- **Eating until the adhan ends.** Stop eating when Fajr begins, not when the call to prayer finishes. A margin of a few minutes is wise.
- **Breaking the fast before Maghrib.** Some phones and apps use a generic time. Use the time for your exact location.
- **Thinking forgetful eating ruins the day.** It does not. Stop as soon as you remember and keep fasting.
- **Skipping suhoor to avoid mistakes.** Suhoor is a blessed sunnah and makes the fast easier. Read [what time to wake up for suhoor](/blog/en/what-time-to-wake-up-for-suhoor/) for a simple plan.

## Summary

- The fast runs from the start of Fajr to the start of Maghrib.
- Eating, drinking and intercourse on purpose, deliberate vomiting, and menstruation break it.
- Forgetful eating, brushing teeth, wudu, showering and saliva do not.
- Ask a trusted scholar about medicine and your own situation.
