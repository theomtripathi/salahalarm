Among the optional prayers, very few have the strong attachment that the two rak'ahs before Fajr have. They are short, they are easy to pray, and they were a steady habit of the Prophet ﷺ. Yet many Muslims are unsure how to pray them, when to begin, and how to fit them around an early alarm.

This guide explains the virtue of the sunnah before Fajr, how to pray it, and how to make it part of a calm morning routine.

## The virtue of the two rak'ahs

Aisha radhiyallahu 'anha said that the Prophet ﷺ did not pay more attention to any optional prayer than to the two rak'ahs of Fajr (Sahih al-Bukhari 1169). This shows how seriously he took them, even though they are only two short rak'ahs.

Sahih Muslim also records a hadith from Aisha in which the Prophet ﷺ said that the two rak'ahs before Fajr are better than the world and all that is in it. The hadith points to the reward of a small act that is kept steady, rather than to the length of the prayer.

## How to pray them

The sunnah before Fajr is two rak'ahs, prayed in the usual way with the opening takbir, recitation, bowing, prostration and the rest of the prayer. The recitation should be light, with the aim being a calm and focused prayer rather than a long one.

Scholars report that the Prophet ﷺ often recited short surahs in these two rak'ahs. Many people choose Surah al-Kafirun in the first rak'ah and Surah al-Ikhlas in the second, as narrated in Sahih Muslim. You may recite whatever you can recite well, and the important thing is that you pray with tranquillity.

## When to pray them

The sunnah is prayed after Fajr has begun, which means after true dawn, and before the obligatory Fajr prayer is established. Pray it at home or in the masjid, and make sure you finish before the iqamah for the obligatory prayer is called.

If the iqamah has already begun when you arrive, do not start the sunnah. Join the congregation for the obligatory prayer. If time remains after the prayer, you may pray the sunnah, and many scholars allow praying it after the sun has risen if you missed it.

## What if I miss it?

Missing the sunnah for a reason is not a disaster. The Prophet ﷺ prayed these two rak'ahs after sunrise on one occasion when he had missed them, so it is recommended to make them up after sunrise and before midday. The obligatory prayer always comes first, so if time is short, pray Fajr and leave the sunnah for later.

## Fitting it around an early alarm

The biggest barrier to the sunnah is the early alarm, not the two rak'ahs themselves. Here is a simple order that many people find helpful:

- Set the alarm for enough time to make wudu and pray calmly.
- Keep your wudu and prayer mat close to where you wake up.
- Pray the two rak'ahs, then the obligatory Fajr prayer.
- Sleep at a regular time, so waking up becomes easier over the weeks.

If you wake with a Fajr alarm, the adhan is the moment to act. Stand up, make wudu, and begin. Our guide on [how to wake up for Fajr](/blog/en/how-to-wake-up-for-fajr/) covers the practical steps in more detail.

## A common mistake

Some people skip the sunnah because they feel rushed, and then they also feel that the whole morning is lost. A better habit is to keep the sunnah short and steady. Two rak'ahs done with calm are far better than a rushed attempt at a longer prayer you cannot maintain.

## Summary

- The two rak'ahs before Fajr are a strongly attached sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.
- Pray them after true dawn, and before the iqamah for the obligatory prayer.
- If you miss them, you may pray them after sunrise, but never delay the obligatory prayer for them.
- Keep the routine simple: alarm, wudu, sunnah, Fajr.
