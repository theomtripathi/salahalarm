A Fajr alarm that does not ring is one of the most frustrating problems a Muslim can face. You set it, you go to bed on time, and then you wake up to a silent phone and a missed prayer. In most cases the fault is a setting, not the app, and it can be fixed in a few minutes.

This guide walks through the checks that fix most cases, in the order that is easiest to test.

## 1. Check the silent switch and the volume

Look at the switch on the side of the phone. On many iPhones, an orange line shows that silent mode is on. Silent mode can silence notification sounds from apps, so turn it off for the night, or make sure the volume is up.

Also remember that alarms from the Clock app and alerts from prayer apps follow different rules. Do not assume that a prayer app will ring just because the Clock app does. Test each one.

## 2. Check Focus and Do Not Disturb

A Focus mode active at night can hide alerts from apps you have not allowed. If you use a sleep Focus, open its settings and add your prayer app to the list of allowed apps. Then run a test with that Focus turned on, because the test is the only way to be sure.

## 3. Check notification permissions for the app

Open the Settings app, find the prayer app, and check that notifications are allowed. Make sure sounds are on, and that alerts are not limited to a quiet delivery. If a notification is delivered silently, you will not hear the adhan or the alarm.

## 4. Keep the app allowed to run in the background

Some alarms need the app to refresh in the background so the next prayer time is ready. Open Settings, go to General, and check Background App Refresh. If it is turned off for your prayer app, turn it on.

## 5. Charge the phone and check the battery

A phone with a low battery can behave differently at night. Make sure the phone is charging when you sleep, and remember that a phone that has been switched off will not ring. Restarting the phone once a week also keeps alarms working smoothly.

## 6. Test the alarm the way you will use it

The best check is a real test. Set an alarm for five or ten minutes ahead, lock the phone, and put it on the charger in the place where it will be at night. Wait for the alarm. If it does not ring, go back through the steps above and try again.

Repeat this test once with each setup you use, such as a sleep Focus or a different charger, because small changes can affect the result.

## When the problem keeps coming back

If the alarm worked for a few days and then stopped, check whether the phone was updated. An update can reset permissions or turn on a new Focus schedule. Look at the settings again after any update.

It also helps to keep a second alarm. Many people set the Clock app alarm for the Fajr time and a prayer app for the adhan. If one fails, the other still wakes you. A second alarm placed across the room is a useful backup for the first week of a new setup.

## Keep the alarm and the prayer together

An alarm only gets you out of bed. The prayer still needs your attention. Once you are awake, make wudu and pray Fajr on time, then decide what to do next. If you keep missing Fajr, read [how to wake up for Fajr](/blog/en/how-to-wake-up-for-fajr/) and [stop missing Fajr](/blog/en/stop-missing-fajr/) for more ideas that do not depend on the phone.

## A simple checklist for tonight

- The silent switch is off.
- The prayer app is allowed in your sleep Focus.
- Notifications are on, with sound.
- Background App Refresh is on for the app.
- The phone is charging.
- You tested the alarm once, with the phone locked.

If all six are true, your Fajr alarm has the best chance of ringing tomorrow. Salah Alarm sets the Fajr adhan as an alarm and stops only when you scan your prayer mat with the phone camera, so you have to get up to end it. Whatever app you use, test it before you rely on it.
