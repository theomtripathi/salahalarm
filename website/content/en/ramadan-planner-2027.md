Ramadan is a month of worship, but many people find that it passes quickly and without a plan. Long days, late nights and family commitments can take the time you meant to spend on prayer and Qur'an. A simple written plan, made before the month begins, is the easiest way to keep the most important things at the centre.

This planner shows you how to set up Ramadan 2027 in advance, with a week-by-week outline that starts in February.

## The key dates

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin on Monday, 8 February 2027. The first taraweeh is on the evening of Sunday, 7 February. Eid al-Fitr is expected on 9 or 10 March 2027, depending on the moon. Check your country's official announcement for the exact days.

## Plan the three stretches

Ramadan has three stretches of ten days. Each has a different focus, so plan them in turn.

**The first ten days** are about settling in. Set your routine for suhoor, taraweeh and sleep, and start your daily reading. Expect the first few days to feel tiring while your body adjusts.

**The middle ten days** are about steadiness. By now the routine should feel normal, so this is the time to keep the pace, catch up on any missed reading, and check your goals.

**The last ten days** are about focus. Plan your week so that the nights of worship are protected. Keep your daily goals small, and add extra time on the odd nights, as the Prophet ﷺ taught us to seek Laylatul Qadr there.

## A daily structure

A simple daily structure keeps the month manageable. Try this outline, and adjust it to your life.

- **Before dawn:** suhoor, a short dua, and Fajr on time.
- **Morning:** a short portion of Qur'an and dhikr.
- **Afternoon:** rest or a short nap, if your body needs it.
- **Iftar:** break the fast with dates and water, then pray Maghrib.
- **Evening:** taraweeh, then a short time with family, then an early night.

## Set your goals in advance

Before the month begins, decide three goals: one for reading the Qur'an, one for a regular act of charity, and one for prayer. Keep them realistic. A goal you can keep every day is worth more than an ambitious goal you abandon after a week.

## Protect your sleep

Ramadan nights are long and tiring, and sleep is the first thing to suffer. Plan a bedtime after taraweeh, keep your phone out of reach, and set your suhoor alarm for the time you have calculated for your city. If you need a practical guide, read [what time to wake up for suhoor](/blog/en/what-time-to-wake-up-for-suhoor/).

## A week-by-week outline

- **Week one:** keep the routine simple. Pray on time, and read a little Qur'an each day.
- **Week two:** add a second reading goal, and check that your sleep is steady.
- **Week three:** review your charity goal, and plan for the first ten-day stretch.
- **Week four:** prepare for the last ten nights by setting your sleep and worship plan.

## Before you start

Plan the month in January, not in February. Write your goals, pick your suhoor routine, and set the alarms for the first week. When Ramadan begins, you will only need to follow the plan.
