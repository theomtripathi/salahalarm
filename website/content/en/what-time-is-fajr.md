If you have ever typed "what time is Fajr" into a search box, you have probably wanted a quick number. The honest answer is that Fajr depends on where you are and what day it is. There is no single time that applies to everyone, and a time you copied from last month may already be wrong.

This guide explains what Fajr means, why its time changes, and how to find the right time for your city.

## What Fajr means

Fajr is the dawn prayer. Its time begins at dawn, when the first light appears across the eastern horizon. It lasts until sunrise. The first light is the faint brightness that spreads before the sun itself rises, and it is what marks the start of the prayer and, in Ramadan, the start of the fast.

Because that first light is a natural event, its exact moment is calculated from the sun's position. Prayer timetables estimate it by setting how far the sun must be below the horizon.

## Why the time changes

The sun's path across the sky changes with the seasons. In most cities, the dawn moves earlier or later by a minute or two each day. Over a year, the difference can be well over an hour in places far from the equator. This is why a fixed alarm time, copied from a note, is often wrong within weeks.

## How to find the time for your city

- **Check your local timetable.** The mosque near you usually publishes a monthly timetable, and it is the most reliable reference for the community.
- **Use a prayer app that calculates Fajr for your location.** It should update the time every day, and let you choose a calculation method.
- **Use the official calendar in your country,** if one is published for the month.

Whichever you use, check it against one source you trust. If the numbers are very different, look at the calculation method first.

## Why apps differ

Different calculation methods use different sun angles to define Fajr. Some use a larger angle, which gives an earlier time. Others are more conservative. Common methods include the Muslim World League, Umm al-Qura, the Egyptian method and the Diyanet method in Turkey. A mosque in your city may use one of these.

So when two apps show different times, the usual reason is that they use different methods. Choose the method your mosque uses, and the times should match.

## What to do with the time

Once you know the time, set your alarm slightly before it, so you have time to make wudu and stand for prayer. Many people set the alarm for a few minutes early, then wake up when the adhan starts.

Salah Alarm is built for this. It calculates Fajr for your location every day, with the method you choose, and it plays the adhan as an alarm that stops only when you scan your prayer mat with your phone camera. You do not need to check the time each morning, because the app works it out for you.

## Why the morning is the hardest prayer to time

Fajr is the prayer most people get wrong, not because the time is hard to find, but because the alarm and the sleep have to match it. The time may be right on the timetable, and still be missed if the alarm is quiet or the phone is across the room with the screen dark. Finding the time is the first step, and waking for it is the second.

## Summary

- It begins at dawn and lasts until sunrise.
