The best suhoor alarm app is really two alarms working together. One wakes you up in time to eat before imsak. The other makes sure that, once you have eaten, you do not fall back asleep and miss Fajr. Most people only set the first one, and that is where Ramadan mornings go wrong.

Ramadan 2027 is expected to start on Monday 8 February. Here is how to set your phone up now so that every suhoor and every Fajr is covered.

## Why suhoor is so easy to sleep through

Suhoor falls in the last part of the night, the deepest stretch of sleep for most people. In Ramadan you are also sleeping later because of taraweeh and late iftars. Put those together and a normal alarm loses: you hear it, turn it off, and wake up at sunrise with no suhoor and no Fajr.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Take suhoor, for in suhoor there is blessing" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1923). It is worth fighting for.

## What a good suhoor alarm setup needs

1. **The right time, every night.** Imsak moves by a minute or so each day and differs from city to city. A fixed 4:30 alarm will be wrong by the end of the month.
2. **Enough time to eat.** Aim to wake 20 to 40 minutes before imsak.
3. **Something that gets you out of bed for Fajr.** After a heavy meal at 5 a.m., the bed is very tempting. This is the step most apps skip.
4. **No distractions.** If you open social media while you eat, Fajr slips past.

## How to set it up with Salah Alarm

Salah Alarm calculates imsak, Fajr and Maghrib for your exact location every day, with the major calculation methods including Umm al-Qura, Muslim World League, ISNA, Egyptian and Diyanet. In Ramadan it gives you three layers:

If you are a very heavy sleeper, add your phone's own alarm 35 to 40 minutes before imsak as a backup, and put the phone across the room.

## A simple suhoor routine for Ramadan 2027

1. **After taraweeh, go straight to bed.** Aim to be asleep before midnight.
2. **Wake at the imsak reminder.** Sit up, drink water and say the waking dua: *Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur* (Sahih al-Bukhari 6312).
3. **Eat a simple suhoor.** Water, eggs or yoghurt, oats or wholegrain bread, and dates. Go easy on salty and very sweet food, which makes you thirsty.
4. **Stop at imsak.** Brush your teeth and make wudu.
5. **Pray Fajr when the alarm rings.** Walk to your prayer mat and scan it to stop the adhan.
6. **Then rest if you need to.**

## Start practising before Ramadan

The habit of waking before dawn is much easier to build in November than on the first night of Ramadan. If you can pray Fajr on time every day for the next three months, suhoor in February will feel normal.
