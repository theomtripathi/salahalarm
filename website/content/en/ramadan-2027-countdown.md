Ramadan 2027 is still a few months away, but it is a good time to start thinking about it. The month arrives quickly, and the people who do best are usually the ones who planned their sleep, their reading and their meals before the first night.

This countdown shows the expected dates, how many days are left from today, and what to prepare in each stage.

## The expected start

Ramadan 1448 is expected to begin on Monday, 8 February 2027. The first night of taraweeh is expected on the evening of Sunday, 7 February. These dates depend on the moon, so your country's official announcement is the final word.

From 7 November 2026, there are about 93 days until 8 February 2027. That gives you a comfortable window to prepare, without rushing.

## November: set your goals

Use November to decide what you want from the month. Write down three goals: one for prayer, one for Qur'an, and one for charity or kindness. Keep each goal small enough that you could keep it on an ordinary busy day. A goal you keep every day is worth more than a large goal you drop after a week.

It also helps to check your current habits. Note the prayers you pray on time and the ones that often slip, and note how much sleep you usually get.

## December and January: build the routine

In the weeks before Ramadan, begin the routine you plan to use in the month. Set a regular bedtime, and try to wake at the same time most mornings. If you plan to eat suhoor, practise waking up for it on a few nights so the first night is not the first time.

This is also a good time to read about the timing. Our guide on [what time to wake up for suhoor](/blog/en/what-time-to-wake-up-for-suhoor/) explains how to plan the meal around your city's times.

## Early February: the final checks

In the last week before the start, check the basics:

- Your alarms for suhoor and Fajr are set, and tested.
- Your prayer times are correct for your city.
- Your reading goal and your daily plan are written down.
- Your family knows your sleep plan and your iftar time.

If you want a fuller outline for the month, read the [Ramadan planner for 2027](/blog/en/ramadan-planner-2027/).

## Plan the three stretches

Ramadan has three stretches of ten days. The first ten days are about settling in. The middle ten are about steadiness. The last ten are about focus, including the odd nights where many people seek Laylatul Qadr. Plan each stretch before it begins, so you are not improvising in the middle of a busy week.

## Keep the countdown simple

You do not need a complicated system. Pick a date in your calendar, add your three goals, and set one alarm for the first suhoor. Then return to the plan once a week until the month begins.
