A salah tracker app does one simple thing: it lets you see, in one place, which prayers you have prayed. That sounds minor, but it changes how people think about their day. A prayer you can see on a list is harder to quietly skip than a prayer you only remember.

This guide explains what a good salah tracker should do, how to use one every day, and how to turn the record into a habit that lasts.

## What a good salah tracker should do

1. **Calculate the right times.** A tracker is only useful if the times are right for your city. Look for one that lets you choose a calculation method, so it matches your mosque.
2. **Make marking a prayer quick.** If marking a prayer takes more than a tap, you will stop doing it after a few days.
3. **Show the past clearly.** A week or a month on one screen helps you see patterns. Without that, the record is just a list of today's taps.
4. **Remind you in time.** A reminder that comes before the prayer, not at the moment it begins, gives you time to prepare.
5. **Stay out of the way.** Ads or endless notifications push people to delete the app. A quiet tracker is one you keep using.

## How to use it every day

Start with the time. Open the app in the morning, check the times for the day, and set your mind on the prayers ahead. Then mark each prayer as you pray it, not later in the evening, when it is easy to forget or to guess.

At the end of the day, take thirty seconds to look at what you have marked. If a prayer is empty, note whether it was missed or simply not recorded. Honest records are more useful than perfect ones.

## Use the record to find your pattern

After a week, look for patterns. Many people find that one prayer is always the weakest. For some it is Fajr because of late nights. For others it is Asr, when work is busiest. Once you see the pattern, change one thing about the hardest prayer rather than trying to fix everything at once.

## What the tracker cannot do for you

A tracker records prayers. It does not wake you up, make you sleep early or make you pray. For Fajr especially, you need an alarm that gets you out of bed and a bedtime that makes getting up possible.

## A one-week test

1. Choose a tracker with accurate times for your city.
2. Mark every prayer on time for seven days, even if you miss one.
3. On day seven, look at the record and find the weakest prayer.
4. Change one thing about that prayer: a reminder, a bedtime or a place to pray.
5. Repeat the test the following week.

## Keep it honest

The point of a tracker is not to look perfect. It is to notice what is happening and to respond. A missed prayer recorded honestly teaches you more than a day of ticks you did not really earn. Pray the missed prayer as soon as you remember, and let the record show what happened.
